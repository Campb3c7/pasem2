Syncope Objectives:

Define syncope and differentiate syncope from presyncope based on the clinical presentation.
Explain the pathophysiology of syncope and identify its most common etiologies.
Compare and contrast the epidemiology, pathophysiology, historical features, and physical exam findings associated with the major classifications of syncope.
Differentiate the major classifications of syncope based on diagnostic evaluation, treatment, and clinical disposition.
Recall the definition and diagnostic testing for orthostatic hypotension.
Identify the three major etiologies for cardiac syncope (structural heart disease, arrhythmia, and cardiopulmonary obstruction) and their associated underlying medical conditions.
Explain the pathophysiology, clinical features and presentation of aortic stenosis and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy that contribute to syncope.
Recognize the EKG findings of atrioventricular (AV) blocks that can cause bradyarrhythmias leading to syncope.
Differentiate a Brugada ECG pattern (Types 1 and 2) from Brugada syndrome.
Describe the pathophysiology, epidemiology, clinical presentation, characteristic ECG findings, and identify appropriate management.
Recognize the pathophysiology, clinical features, characteristic ECG findings, and management of long QT syndrome.
Identify medications and electrolyte abnormalities known to cause prolonged QT interval and recognize their potential to increase the risk of ventricular arrhythmias and syncope.
Recognize the clinical presentation and characteristic EKG findings of Wolff-Parkinson-White (WPW) Syndrome.
Recognize the clinical features of polymorphic ventricular tachycardia.
Describe the pathophysiology and clinical presentation of cardiopulmonary conditions that may cause syncope, including pulmonary embolism, myocardial infarction, aortic dissection, and cardiac tamponade.
Differentiate syncope from seizure using patient history, associated symptoms, and findings during and after the event.
Apply a systematic approach to syncope evaluation, including focused history-taking, physical exam, orthostatic vital signs, ECG interpretation, and selection of additional diagnostic testing when indicated.
Identify high-risk historical features, physical exam findings, and ECG abnormalities associated with potentially life-threatening causes of syncope.
Differentiate patients with syncope who are appropriate for outpatient management from those who require emergency evaluation, observation, or hospital admission based on high-risk clinical features and suspected etiology.

Inpatient & Long-Term Arrhythmia Management Objectives:

Review the risk factors and triggers of atrial fibrillation. 
Describe the epidemiology, pathophysiology, and remodeling of the atria that occur in atrial fibrillation. Recall the classification of atrial fibrillation.
Detail the clinical presentation, workup, and management of atrial fibrillation. Compare and contrast the advantages and disadvantages of pursuing a rhythm control vs a rate control strategy in atrial fibrillation.
Briefly describe the technique for performing cardioversion, radiofrequency catheter ablation, and the maze procedure. 
List the following anti-arrhythmic drugs by trade and generic name and if listed below, recall the following principles of treatment.
Amiodarone (Pacerone, Cordarone)- especially useful in persistent AF, drug of choice in atrial fib for patients with cardiac disease-CAD or systolic or diastolic heart failure
Dronedarone (Multaq): contraindicated in recent HF or severe NYHA class HF, permanent AF
Sotalol (Betapace): contraindicated in patients with HF or prolonged QT
Dofetilide (Tikosyn): must be started in the hospital, used in patients with HF, CAD, CVA, HTN who also have atrial fibrillation
Discuss the risk of stroke in non-valvular AF and the factors which influence this risk. Describe the difference in stroke risk in paroxysmal vs persistent vs permanent atrial fibrillation. 
Describe the utility of the CHADS2-VASc scoring system in determining the drug of choice for embolic stroke prevention. Describe the utility of the HAS-BLED scoring system to determine the risk of major bleed in patients treated with warfarin. 
Recall the elements of the CHADS2-VASc scoring system and if given a scenario, be able to calculate a CHADS2-VASc score and know how to apply this information. 
Compare and contrast the limitations, risks, and benefits of anticoagulation with warfarin and the DOACs. Know the following DOACs by trade and generic name: dabigitran, rivaroxaban, and apixaban. 
Review the management of the hospital patient with NSVT and the indications for more extensive testing as well as the elements of the work-up.
Discuss the evaluation of a patient with persistent sinus tachycardia and review the syndromes of inappropriate sinus tachycardia and POTS.
