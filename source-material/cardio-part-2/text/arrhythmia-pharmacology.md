  - Indications:

## Slide 11

- Adenosine
  - Dosing
    - Initial Dose: 6 mg IV
    - If Ineffective (1-2 mins): 12 mg IV (may repeat once)

    - MOA: Slows AV node conduction, interrupts re-entry pathways, restores NSR
  - Onset and Duration
    - Onset: Immediate
    - Half-life: < 10 seconds

## Slide 13

- Adenosine: How it works
  - Mechanism of Action
    - Effect: Directly inhibits AV nodal conduction, increases AV nodal refractory period
  - Cellular Action
    - Potassium Channels: Activated, driving K+ out of cells (main effect)
    - Calcium Influx: Inhibited, disrupting resting potential
  - Resulting Effects
    - Longer depolarization time - slowed conduction in cardiac myocytes

  - Primary Uses:

## Slide 28

- Class I: Sodium Channel Blockers
  - Subdivisions based on Rate of Dissociation During Diastole:
    - IA (Intermediate): Procainamide, Quinidine, Disopyramide
    - IB (Rapid): Lidocaine, Mexiletine
    - IC (Slow): Flecainide, Propafenone
  - Additional Properties
    - IA and IC: Also block potassium channels
      - Effect: Slows repolarization, prolongs action potential
  - Class IC Specifics
    - Slow Dissociation: Flecainide, Propafenone
      - Result: Increased effect at faster rates (use-dependence)
      - Risk: Higher proarrhythmic activity, especially in diseased myocardium

## Slide 35

- Flecainide (Tambocor): Warnings
- US Boxed Warnings
  - Proarrhythmic Effects:
    - Increased PVCs, V tach, V fib, and death reported in atrial fibrillation patients
    - Not recommended for chronic atrial fibrillation
  - Atrial Flutter:
    - 1:1 AV conduction risk
    - Use preemptive negative chronotropic therapy (e.g., digoxin, beta-blockers)
- Dietary Considerations
  - Milk Interaction:
    - Decreases absorption of flecainide
    - Adjust dose if milk is removed from diet
    - Monitor serum levels with changes in milk intake

## Slide 40

- Beta Blocker Toxicity
  - Attempted suicide or accidental overdose with beta blockers can result in bradycardia, hypotension, low cardiac output, cardiac failure, and cardiogenic shock
  - Bronchospasm & respiratory depression may also occur due to severe circulatory impairment or a central drug effect.
  - The myocardium in severe intoxications may become relatively refractory to pharmacologic & electrical stimulation, resulting in asystolic death.
  - Changes in mental status, convulsions, and coma have also been described

## Slide 42

- Class III: Potassium Channel Blockers
- Mechanism of Action
  - Block Rapid Potassium Current:
    - Prolong refractory period
    - Prolong action potential duration (APD)
    - No effect on phase 0 depolarization or resting membrane potential
  - Effects on Myocytes
    - Decreased excitability
    - Prevents reentry mechanisms and ectopic foci
    - Reduces tachyarrhythmias
  - Included Medications
    - Amiodarone (Cordarone, Pacerone)
    - Sotalol (Betapace)
    - Dronedarone (Multaq)
    - Ibutilide (Corvert)
    - Dofetilide (Tikosyn)
