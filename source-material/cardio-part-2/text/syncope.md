## Slide 15

- Trigger occurs: prolonged standing, fear, or seeing blood
- Initial response: Increased sympathetic activity
- Reflex vagal activation: The autonomic nervous system overcorrects, increasing parasympathetic activity and decreasing sympathetic activity
- BP drops: Blood vessels dilate, and HR may slow
- Temporary decreased blood flow to the brain
- How does reflex syncope work?
- Syncope: Patient faints

> Notes: Key takeaway: The autonomic nervous system overreacts to a trigger, causing blood pressure to drop (and sometimes heart rate to drop), temporarily reducing blood flow to the brain.

## Slide 16

- Reflex Syncope
- Includes vasovagal syncope, situational syncope, and carotid sinus syndrome
- Reflex syncope accounts for 90% of cases among younger adults but only 40% of cases among older adults
- Most common type of syncope
- Mechanism:
  - Vasodilation and/or bradycardia  Systemic hypotension  Cerebral hypoperfusion  Fainting

> Notes: Vasovagal syncope usually results from: either, systolic blood pressure of no more than 80 mmHg, heart rate of < 40 bpm, asystole lasting at least 3 seconds

## Slide 27

- Orthostatic Syncope
- Mechanism:
  - Change to upright posture  blood shifts to lower body  cardiac output drops
  - Inadequate compensation (autonomic failure, volume depletion or drug SE)  cerebral hypoperfusion  syncope
- Orthostatic hypotension is defined as a sustained reduction of systolic BP ≥ 20 mmHg or diastolic BP ≥  10 mmHg within 3 minutes of standing.

## Slide 62

- Autosomal dominant genetic disorder
- Epidemiology:
  - Majority are of Southeast Asian descent, M > F
  - Typically diagnosed in adulthood
  - Brugada Syndrome less prevalent than Brugada Pattern (EKG findings only)

## Slide 63

- Brugada Pattern vs. Brugada Syndrome
- Brugada Pattern:
  - Typical EKG features
  - Asymptomatic with no other clinical criteria
- Brugada Syndrome:
  - Typical EKG features
  - History of sustained ventricular tachyarrhythmia, sudden cardiac death, or other associated clinical criteria (such as syncope)

## Slide 64

- Taking Pt History for Special Populations
- Elderly:
  - Common issues: physiological problems, cardiovascular disease
  - Aortic stenosis is a common structural issue
  - Always review meds – check for meds that can cause bradycardia, orthostatic hypotension, QT prolongation
- Children and adolescents:
  - Assess if syncope is related to activity or sports
  - Possible causes: HOCM, septal defects, breath holding
  - Family history – heart disease, QT prolongation, arrhythmia, sudden cardiac death
