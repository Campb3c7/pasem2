## Slide 14

- Diagnosis of Surgical Complications
- Bleeding/Hematoma-When significant it is usually obvious with mass or visible blood.  Also hypotension/tachycardia
- Pseudoaneurysm-Pulsatile mass
- Emboli/Thrombus/StenosisOcclusionLimb Ischemia-Loss of pulses and acute/chronic ischemic changes (pain, pallor etc.)

## Slide 15

- Therapy for Surgical Complications
- Bleeding/Hematoma-If in PACU probably needs rapid return to OR.
- Pseudoaneurysm-Typically needs repaired
- Thrombus/Stenosis/Occlusion-Typically needs dilated or redone
- Limb ischemia-Typically needs an intervention
- Emboli-Embolectomy and where did it originate?

## Slide 16

- Peripheral Vascular Disease
- Follow-up
- Watch for return of symptoms
- Measurement of ABI at rest and after exercise
- Regular screening with Duplex U/S-every 3 months. (Intervention based on duplex surveillance protocol leads to significantly higher 5 year patency rates)
- Oral anticoagulants and/or antiplatelet therapy long term post-op

## Slide 17

- Peripheral Vascular Disease
- Peripheral Vascular Surgery is a temporizing measure.
- If the patient does not change their lifestyle the bypass, the angioplasty, the stent will ultimately fail.
- This just buys them time.

## Slide 22

- CAROTID DISEASE
- Symptomatic Disease:
- Contralateral focal neurologic symptoms within 6 months in distribution of internal Carotid
- Ipsilateral Monocular blindness (Amaurosis Fugax)
- Ipsilateral focal findings on brain CT or MRI
- Memory and Thinking problems(?)
- NOT vertigo or syncope

> Notes: Monocular blindness - Amaurosis Fugax
> Due to emboli to ophthalmic artery

## Slide 25

- Carotid Disease Treatment
- Stenting and Surgical-CEA
- Symptomatic and 70-99% stenosis
- Asymptomatic and 80-99% Stenosis
- Surgically accessible lesion
- Absence of severe cardiac/pulmonary/systemic disease
- Non-debilitating stroke
- Life expectancy > 5 years.

## Slide 26

- Carotid Disease Treatment
- Why not operate on everyone with stenotic disease?
- Carotid surgery is prophylactic
- Stroke Risk:
- Operative (1-3%)
- Non-operative (dependent on the degree of stenosis)
- DO NOT OPERATE
- ***Total Occlusion of Carotid***

- Surgical Complications
- Complications related to carotid surgery:

- Chemoreceptor – Carotid Body
- Hematoma
- Stroke (1-3%)
- Cerebral Hyperperfusion Syndrome (Headaches, seizures, focal neurologic deficits)

> Notes: Carotid Sinus controls BP
> Carotid Body chemoreceptors sensitive to PO2 and CO2, pH and temp
> Cerebral Hyperperfusion syndrome - H/A, seizures, focal neurologic deficits

## Slide 37

- What To Do?
- Complications related to carotid surgery:
- BP Lability – Monitor in ICU/Step-down
- Hematoma—Rapid return to OR
- Stroke (1-3%)—Supportive Care
- Cerebral Hyperperfusion Syndrome—BP Control; Edema control; Anticonvulsants

> Notes: Carotid Sinus controls BP
> Carotid Body chemoreceptors sensitive to PO2 and CO2, pH and temp
> Cerebral Hyperperfusion syndrome - H/A, seizures, focal neurologic deficits

## Slide 38

- Surgical Complications
- Complications related to carotid surgery:
  - Nerve injury
  - Vagus
  - Recurrent Laryngeal
  - Hypoglossal
  - Sympathetic Nerves
  - Horner Syndrome (Miosis, Ptosis, Anhydrosis)

> Notes: Vagus nerve injury leads to abd pain, acid reflux, changes to HR, BP, difficulty swallowing, hoarseness, N/V
> Hypoglossal nerve injury leads to tongue deviation to the contralateral side
