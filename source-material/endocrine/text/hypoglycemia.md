## Slide 3

- Glucose Homeostasis
- In the non-diabetic, healthy patient, glucose levels are maintained from fasting and pre-prandial levels <100 mg/dL to post-prandial levels <140 mg/dL, by multiple hormones.
- Insulin: a pancreatic hormone
- Suppresses glucose production by the liver
- Encourages glycogen storage
- Promotes peripheral glucose utilization by tissues

## Slide 4

- Glucose Homeostasis
- Four hormones are released to counteract hypoglycemia:
- Glucagon: stimulates breakdown of hepatic glycogen and creation of glucose
- Epinephrine: suppresses insulin, promotes breakdown of glycogen and fat for energy
- Cortisol and 4. growth hormone: enhance glucagon’s effect and stimulate lipolysis, ketogenesis and proteolysis

- Symptoms of Hypoglycemia(Generally, begin at glucose levels of 50-60mg/dL)
- Neurogenic: mediated by catecholamines
  - Nervousness/anxiety/ irritability
  - Diaphoresis
  - Tremor
  - Tachycardia
  - Pallor
  - Hunger
  - Palpitations
  - Nausea/Vomiting

## Slide 7

- Symptoms of Hypoglycemia(Generally begin at glucose levels of 50-60mg/dL)
- Neuroglycopenic: due to lack of glucose to the brain
  - Weakness/ tiredness
  - Slow mentation/ confusion
  - Headache
  - Syncope/ pre-syncope
  - Blurred vision
  - Abnormal behavior
  - Seizures
  - Amnesia/ LOC/ coma / death (relatively rare)

## Slide 8

- Etiologies of Hypoglycemia
- Drugs: (many others are possible)
- Insulins: check the duration of action
- Sulfonylureas: long duration of action, initial hypoglycemia can recur following treatment.
- Meglitinides: fairly short duration of action
- Alcohol: suppresses gluconeogensis but not glycolysis, so patients with limited glycogen stores or those on a prolonged EtOH binge are most vulnerable.

## Slide 9

- Etiologies of Hypoglycemia
- Severe illness: multi-system organ failure, particularly hepatic/ renal failure; shock; sepsis and malnutrition
- Endocrine disorders: hypopituitarism, Addison’s disease, myxedema coma
- If none of the above, consider rare causes
  - Hyper-insulinism (B cell tumor, islet hyperplasia)
  - Non-insulin producing, extra pancreatic tumor (IGF-2 release)

## Slide 10

- Ddx: Dumping Syndrome
- Symptoms of dumping syndrome were previously ascribed to hypoglycemia and are similar to hypoglycemic symptoms.
- Occurs within 1-2 hours after a meal
- Often seen after GI surgery, particularly partial gastrectomy or gastric bypass
- Symptoms include palpitations, tachycardia, lightheadedness, diaphoresis and orthostasis.

## Slide 11

- Ddx: Dumping Syndrome
- Most patients do not actually develop hypoglycemia on labs when symptomatic.
- Instead, symptoms are most likely due to a rapid discharge of ingested carbohydrate into the small bowel.
- This sudden osmotic load results in a rapid shift of fluid from the vasculature to the gut lumen, causing symptoms.

## Slide 12

- Clinical Hypoglycemia
- Low levels of blood glucose may occur for the above etiologies transiently but hypoglycemia is not considered to be an illness unless Whipple’s triad is satisfied:
- Symptoms are consistent with hypoglycemia
- Hypoglycemia is present when the symptoms occur
- Administration of glucose leads to symptom improvement

## Slide 13

- Work-up of Hypoglycemia
- If s/s not currently present at the time of evaluation, we can provoke them.
  - Fasting Evaluation
  - Post Prandial Evaluation
- Labs ordered in the setting of a 72 hour fast:
  - Plasma glucose, Insulin, C-peptide, Proinsulin Beta-Hydroxybutyrate (BHOB), drug levels (Sulfonylurea, Meglitinide), Cortisol, Growth Hormone

## Slide 15

- Treatment of Hypoglycemia
- If the pt is alert, candy, glucose tablet, or
- orange juice
- If the pt is stuporous or any LOC:
  - 50 mL of 50% dextrose (D50) given rapid IV or
  - Glucagon 1mg IM or SC in buttock, arm or thigh, follow instructions on kit. Intranasal glucagon is also available.
- Medic Alert jewelry
- Glucagon emergency kit and instructions to family and friends
