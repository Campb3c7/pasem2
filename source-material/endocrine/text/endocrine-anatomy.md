## Slide 4

- Hypothalamus
- Portion of the brain that maintains homeostasis
- Link between the endocrine and nervous systems via pituitary gland
- Produces releasing hormones that stimulate or inhibit the secretion of other hormones from the pituitary gland
- Helps control heart rate/blood pressure, body temperature, fluid & electrolyte balance (including thirst), appetite and body weight, glandular secretions of stomach/intestines, influences pituitary to release hormones, fatigue and sleep cycles

> Notes: Help make hormones that control other parts of endocrine system

## Slide 5

- Hypothalamus
- Located below the thalamus but above the pituitary gland
- Tells the anterior pituitary to synthesize its own hormones
- Primary stimulating hormones:
  - Thyrotropin releasing hormone (TRH)
  - Corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH)
  - Gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH)
  - Growth hormone-releasing (GHRH)
- Primary inhibiting hormones:
  - Somatostatin/Growth hormone-inhibiting hormone (GHIH)
  - Dopamine/Prolactin-inhibiting hormone (PIH)

> Notes: Stimulating = releasing hormoes

## Slide 7

- Pituitary Gland(aka – Hypophysis Cerebri)
- “Master gland” of the Endocrine System
- Located in the “Sella Turcica” of the Sphenoid bone just below the optic chiasm
- Connected to the hypothalamus via the infundibulum (stalk)
- Consists of two separate lobes – anterior and posterior (each of which has separate functions and controls)

## Slide 8

- Pituitary Gland
- Anterior:
  - Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH)
  - Adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH)
  - Follicle stimulating hormone (FSH)
  - Luteinizing hormone (LH)
  - Growth hormone (GH)
  - Prolactin
- Posterior:
  - Oxytocin
  - Vasopressin (ADH)

## Slide 9

- Anterior Pituitary
- Stimulating hormones
- TRH  TSH  Thyroid hormone
- CRH  ACTH  Cortisol
- GnRH  FSH and LH  Sex hormones (Estrogen, Progesterone, Testosterone) and helps with maturation of gametes
- GHRH  GH  Bone and tissue growth

> Notes: When thyroid hormones increases, then it sends an inhibitory response so halts production when needed

- Prolactin inhibiting hormone  X Prolactin
- During breastfeeding, it sends a message to the brain to halt production of PIH thus inhibiting it and allowing production of Prolactin

## Slide 15

- Sella Turcica
- Saddle shaped depression in sphenoid bone
- Three parts
  - Tuberculum sellae (horn of saddle)
    - Anterior boundary of the hypophysial fossa
  - Hypophysial fossa (pituitary fossa)
    - Median depression in the body of sphenoid that accommodates the pituitary gland
  - Dorsum sellae (back of saddle)
    - Square plate of bone projecting superiorly from the body of the sphenoid that forms the posterior boundary of the sella turcica
    - Posterior clinoid processes on each side

## Slide 17

- Optic Chiasm
- NOTE: floor of sella turcica is very thin next to the sphenoid sinus. Infection of the sinus can cause disruption of this bony layer causing problems with the pituitary. Also, note the roof is narrow where the “infundibulum” of the pituitary doesn’t allow for expansion of any tumors of the pituitary.

## Slide 18

- Sella Turcica
- Clinical significance
  - Sinus invasion
    - Infection of the sphenoid sinus can impact the pituitary gland during infection
  - Pituitary tumor
    - The tumor usually extends upward and can result in compression of the optic chiasm
      - This leads to bitemporal hemianopsia  pathognomonic for a pituitary tumor

## Slide 20

- Cavernous sinus
- Clinical significance
  - The pituitary gland lies between the paired sinuses
  - An abnormal pituitary adenoma expands into the cavernous sinus

- Pineal Gland
- Small endocrine gland in the brain
- Located in epithalamus near the center of the brain between two hemispheres
- Very small pinecone shaped gland
- May appear calcified in radiographs in older people due to deposits of fluoride, calcium, & phosphorus

## Slide 24

- Adrenal Glands(aka – suprarenal glands)
- Located on top of each kidney (hence “suprarenal”)
- Right adrenal is more “pyramidal shaped”, while left is “crescent shaped”
- Separated from kidneys by a thin septum
  - Important for renal transplants
- Enclosed by renal fascia by which are attached to the crura of the diaphragm
- Each gland has an inner medulla and outer cortex, each of which have different endocrine functions
- Each gland has a hilum where veins and lymphatics exit, but the arterial and nerve supply enter at various sites

## Slide 25

- Adrenal Glands

## Slide 26

- Adrenal Glands
- Suprarenal cortex
  - Secretes aldosterone, cortisol, and precursors for sex hormones
    - Aldosterone causes the kidneys to retain sodium and water in response to stress, increasing the blood volume and blood pressure
    - Cortisol secreted during “fight or flight” situations
- Suprarenal medulla
  - Chromaffin cells of medulla are related to sympathetic ganglion neurons
    - Secrete catecholamines (mostly epinephrine) into the bloodstream in response to signals from presynaptic neurons
    - Epinephrine and norepinephrine active the body to a flight-or fight status in response to traumatic stress
      - Increase HR and BP, dilate bronchioles, and change blood flow patterns

## Slide 27

- Adrenal Glands
- Arterial supply is VERY abundant
  - Due to endocrine functional needs
- The suprarenal arteries branch freely before entering the gland so that 50-60 branches penetrate the capsule covering the surface of gland
- Arterial supply:
  - Superior suprarenal- from inferior phrenic arteries
  - Middle suprarenal- from the abdominal aorta near the level of celiac trunk
  - Inferior suprarenal- from the renal arteries

## Slide 30

- Adrenal Glands
- Venous return is via suprarenal veins – right empties into the IVC and the left into the left renal vein.
- Rich nerve supply from celiac plexus and abdominopelvic splanchnic nerves supply the cortex, while presynaptic sympathetic fibers (mainly from spinal cord levels T10-L1) supply the medulla.

## Slide 31

- Pancreas
- An elongated, accessory digestive gland
- Lies posterior to the stomach between the duodenum on the right and spleen on the left
- Both exocrine (digestion) and endocrine (insulin & glucagon)  functions
- When blood sugar is high, beta cells secrete insulin into the blood to help cells take in glucose and use for energy (lowering blood sugar)
- When blood sugar is low, alpha cells secrete glucagon which binds to receptors on the liver activating a breakdown of glycogen which releases glucose into the bloodstream cells to be used between meals

## Slide 32

- Pancreas
- Divided into 4 parts:
  - Head of the pancreas lives in the C of the duodenum and uncinate process (projection of the head) extends posteriorly to the superior mesenteric artery (SMA)
  - The neck overlies the superior mesenteric vessels and adjacent to the pylorus of the stomach
  - The body lies to the left of the superior mesenteric vessels passing over the aorta and L2 vertebra
  - The tail lies anterior to the left kidney and projects toward the LUQ and the spleen

## Slide 34

- Pancreas
- Main pancreatic duct
  - Begins at the tail of the pancreas and runs through the gland to the head
  - The pancreatic duct and bile duct unite to form the hepatopancreatic ampulla (of Vater) which opens into the duodenum
  - Its controlling sphincter is called the Sphincter of Oddi
- The sphincter is smooth muscle and controls the flow of bile and pancreatic juice into the ampulla and prevents reflux of duodenal content into the ampulla
- The accessory pancreatic duct joins with the main pancreatic duct

## Slide 35

- Pancreas
- Arterial supply
  - Mainly from branches of splenic artery (body and tail) and branches from gastroduodenal and superior mesenteric arteries (head and neck)
- Venous drainage
  - Occurs through the pancreatic veins into the splenic and superior mesenteric veins that empty into the hepatic portal system
- Nervous supply from vagus and splanchnic passing through the diaphragm

- Thyroid Gland
- Body’s largest endocrine gland
- Affects all areas of the body except for itself, spleen, testes, and uterus
- Produces:
  - Thyroid hormone
    - Controls rate of metabolism
  - Calcitonin

## Slide 41

- Thyroid Gland
- Located anteriorly in the neck at C5-T1
- Consists mostly of left and right lobes, anterolateral to the larynx and trachea
- The isthmus joins the lobes over the trachea just inferior to the cricoid cartilage of larynx over the tracheal rings
- Lobes project superiorly from there on the lateral aspect of the thyroid cartilage of the larynx

## Slide 42

- Thyroid Gland
- Gland is surrounded by a thin fibrous capsule and dense connective tissue attaches the capsule to the cricoid cartilage  and tracheal rings
- External to the capsule is a loose sheath formed by visceral portion of the pretracheal layer of deep cervical fascia
- May or may not be palpable, but if is – is consistency of muscle.

## Slide 49

- Parathyroid glands
- Small flattened, oval glands (4 of them)
- Located on the posterior surface of the thyroid gland on right and left lobes (superior and inferior) on the external capsule
- Participate in calcium level regulation (acts on bone directly to release more CA2+ into bloodstream)

## Slide 50

- Parathyroid Glands
- Blood supply – usually branches of the inferior thyroid artery but can vary. Parathyroid veins drain into thyroid venous plexus
- Lymphatic vessels – drain into thyroid lymph vessels which drain into deep cervical nodes
- Nerve supply – abundant; derived from thyroid branches of the cervical sympathetic ganglia

## Slide 52

- Gonads (Ovaries)
- Oval shaped about the size of a large grape
- Located on either side of the uterus attached to the fimbria
- Hormones: Estrogens (Estradiol, Estrone, Estriol) & Progesterone
- Controlled by the FSH & LH

## Slide 53

- Gonads (Testes)
- Twin Oval-shaped organs about the size of a large grape
- Located in scrotum to aid in temperature control for sperm production
- Testosterone is the hormone necessary for physical development in boys, growth of body and facial hair, lowering of voice, increase in height & muscle mass; maintains libido, sperm production, & bone density
- Testosterone production controlled by FSH and LH
