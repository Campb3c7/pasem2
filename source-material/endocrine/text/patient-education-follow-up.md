## Slide 7

- Getting the Patient Involved
- “Increasing levels of literacy and development of mass media in recent years have encouraged recipients of healthcare to demand more information about their illness process and care plans.”
- Well educated patients understand and manage their own health and medical care better
- Education promotes patient compliance, satisfaction with care, healthy lifestyles, and self-care skills

> Notes: Education is used to empower patients and is an important aspect of quality improvement, given that it is associated with improved health outcomes.
> 
> In addition, patient education has also helped to decrease unpleasant patient experiences in hospitals, including reduced levels of pain and anxiety 
> 
> Patient education reduces readmissions and ER visits, saves money. Chelsey Ledue. Patients who have a clear understanding of their after-hospital care instructions are 30 percent less likely to be readmitted or visit the emergency department than patients who lack this information, according to a new study funded by the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality.

## Slide 8

- What’s Included?
- Basic skills
  - Identify patient’s educational needs, information about patient’s daily activities, health beliefs, level of understanding and preferred language.
  - Tailor education to the patient’s educational level and cultural background
  - Encourage questions
  - Utilize appropriate written, audiovisual, and computer-based materials

>

## Slide 9

- What’s Included?
- Short-term plans for acute illness
  - Prepare patient for symptoms and effects of condition, examination, and/or treatment.
  - Assess patient’s ability to carry out treatment plan; identify barriers and personalize treatment accordingly
  - Assess patient’s understanding of the education by having them restate the treatment plan
  - Document educational efforts in specific terms in the record

> Notes: https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2000/1001/p1712.html#:~:text=Identify%20patient's%20educational%20needs.,of%20findings%20clearly%20and%20concisely.

## Slide 10

- What’s Included?
- Long-term strategies for chronic disease
  - Provide patient with understandable explanation of their disease state and what that means for them.
  - Involve patient in setting treatment goals and the treatment plan
  - Provide opportunities for patient to discuss feelings
  - Assess patient's background, home and work environment and adapt education and plan accordingly
  - Document educational efforts in specific terms in the record

> Notes: Provide patient with adequate feedback on their progress toward goals
> Present manageable amounts of information to the patient over time

## Slide 11

- What’s Included?
- Health promotion
  - Determine patient’s health-risk behaviors
  - Introduce health promoting topics, assessing patient’s priorities and readiness to change health-related behaviors
  - Motivational interviewing
  - Enlist assistance of other health care professionals (nutritionist, nurses, athletic trainers)
- Incorporation of patient education in practice
  - Evaluate commercial education resources, such as brochures, books, and internet materials
  - Develop systems to involve office staff in assisting with patient education
  - Utilize family conferences when appropriate
  - Be aware of emerging technologies

## Slide 15

- Patient Follow Up
- Follow up should be a core topic discussed at the end of every medical encounter.
- Can help provider identify areas of misunderstanding, answer questions, make further assessments, and adjust treatments.
- Follow up also helps promote a good relationship between provider and patient.
- Appropriate follow up has been shown to help reduce risk of readmission to the hospital.

> Notes: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0200691
> 
> Try to classify patients based on their overall picture as you go into the decision of when to bring back for follow up:  Younger? Older? Comorbidities? Otherwise healthy? Lots of medications? Good home care/support/resources? Trustworthy?

## Slide 16

- Patient Follow Up
- Timing of follow up should be thought of on a spectrum based on:
  - The medical environment the patient is in
  - The conditions they have
  - The type of follow up that is needed
- Patients need specific instructions on when, where, and why they need to seek follow up care.
- Identify who the patient will follow up with and how (i.e. the format of the follow up- office visit, phone, MyChart))

> Notes: Reasons for follow up: Monitor health, reinforce knowledge and action plans, confirm medicine regimens, schedule appts, verify follow through on referrals, share lab results
> Ask patients to record information
> 
> 
> BE SURE TO SPECIFY IF THEY, THE PATIENT, NEED TO SET UP THE APPT OR NOT

## Slide 17

- Patient Follow Up – Inpatient Discharge
- Inpatient environment
  - Typically recommends follow up in 1 week with PCP post hospital stay
      - Exceptions: Inpatient warfarin initiation, uncommon/challenging med changes have recommendation for sooner follow up with PCP
  - Specialists: the discharging hospitalist will often contact specialist to see when they want to see them in office outpatient after discharge.
    - Typically, specialists will request 2-4 weeks timeline to follow up with the patient after being discharged from the hospital
      - Exception: Decompensated heart failure typically has cardiologist follow up within a week
  - If hospitalist wants labs done post discharge request will typically involve getting lab work done before follow up with PCP or specialist

## Slide 18

- Patient Follow Up – ER Discharge
- Emergency environment
- Typically, recommends follow up in 48 hours with PCP
- If specialist is involved in follow up: On average 1-2 weeks, unless they deem follow up sooner is recommended.
  - Exceptions: Urology (longer wait)
  - Emergency rooms and specialists have contracts that the specialist office will see ER discharges “within a timely manner”.
  - Patient’s leave the ER and call the specialist office to schedule an appt
- ER providers always give specific details to the patient on returning to the ER for care.

## Slide 20

- Patient Follow Up – Primary Care
- Acute Visit Follow Up:
    - 24-48 hours:  ____________________________________
    - 3-5 days: ________________________________________
    - 1-2 weeks: ______________________________________
    - “Follow up as needed if symptoms are not improving, resolving, or if they worsen.” “Due back for annual physical exam in ** months.”
    - Chronic Care Follow Up:
      - Typically, a 3,6, or 12-month follow up pattern
      - More complex the patient the shorter the follow up timeframe

> Notes: 24-48 hours: Concerning acute condition that doesn’t warrant ER but deems very close follow up to assure condition improvement.
> Consider patient’s wishes here too.
> Example:  Acute respiratory illnesses- patients who are struggling or have multiple comorbidities
> 
> Follow up in 3-5 days 
> Usually assessing improvement or resolution of a higher acuity illness or after completion of a medication treatment.
> Example: Recheck a cellulitis in patients with risk factors for complications to assure it is improving.
> 
> Follow up in 1-2 weeks
> Assessing improvement or resolution of a less severe illness or after completion of a medication treatment.
> Examples: 
> Rechecking viral gastroenteritis in patients that are of concern if they can’t keep medicine or food down for extended period, making sure symptoms have improved.
> Rechecking an acute MSK complaint after trialing RICE and NSAIDs
> Rechecking constipation after trial on Miralax
> 
> PRN- This is to be used in that patient that is most likely to do completely fine in their treatment and you trust will return if they are not getting better.
> The patients that have little to no risk of not healing up well.
> I will often add in my note documentation to the patient “If we do not hear from you, we will assume your condition resolved.”
> Examples: Common cold in the otherwise healthy individual, strep throat, uncomplicated ear infections.
> 
> Chronic
> How many chronic conditions do they have? Examples: Diabetes, Hypertension, Hyperlipidemia, COPD,  Asthma, Hypothyroidism, Depression,  Anxiety, etc.
> Age? 
> How many medications are they on? Were any new medications initiated?
> How often do they need labs drawn?
> Are their chronic conditions well controlled or uncontrolled?
> What kind of patient is it, motivated or unmotivated?
> 
> 3-month follow ups:
> Multiple chronic conditions, typically uncontrolled, older age, with multiple medications.
> 
> 6-month follow ups:
> 1-3 chronic conditions, well controlled or very mildly uncontrolled in a motivated patient. Middle aged or younger.
> 
> 12-month follow ups:
> Younger patients with one or more chronic conditions, well controlled and stable.

- Patient Follow Up – Primary Care
- Preventative visits
- Medicare Annual Wellness Exam
  - Medicare term for an annual visit in their patient population. Completely free for patient.
  - Medicare does not pay for an “annual physical”
  - Medicare HRA questionnaire provided to patient→ they answer prior to visit → give to nurse/MA at time of visit and answers are recorded into the computer → Provider is then responsible for going over any individual responses that are “flagged” by Medicare as “attention needed” with documentation in the note and patient education of issues addressed at the end of the visit.

## Slide 24

- Patient Follow Up – Primary Care
- New Problem Follow Up Visits
  - Visits that follow up on acute/subacute problem visit. Follow up time frames can vary significantly just based on individual patient and problem.
    - Psychiatric complaints (prior to chronic condition diagnosis): 1-2 week follow up for patients with acute psychiatric difficulties, 4-6 week follow up with SSRI/SNRI initiation, sooner as needed.
    - ENT complaints: A large variation in follow up times, just dependent on the complaint
    - MSK complaints: typically, 2-4 week follow ups

> Notes: MSK complaints: typically, 2-4 week follow ups
> Psychiatric complaints (prior to chronic condition diagnosis): 1-2 week follow up for patients with acute psychiatric difficulties, 4-6 week follow up with SSRI/SNRI initiation, sooner as needed.
> GI complaints: Huge variation, shorter term follow ups for more distressing symptoms, longer term follow ups for less distressing symptoms/chronic GERD
> ENT complaints: Also, large variation in follow ups, just dependent on particular complaints
> Etc.

## Slide 25

- Patient Follow Up – Primary Care
- Hospital follow ups
  - TCM (Transitional Care Management)
    - Communication: Initial contact with patient required within 2 business days of hospital discharge
    - Medical decision making of moderate to high complexity during the service period
    - Face to face visit within 7-14 days of discharge
  - Purpose of these visits are to ease transition from inpatient to outpatient and avoid readmission.
  - Review of hospital documentation, updating chart, assuring patient is improving and closely monitoring through their personal recovery.
  - Follow up from these visits should be decided on patient’s acute/subacute problem that hospitalized them alongside their chronic conditions and overall picture.

> Notes: Small outpatient procedures can be performed in the family practice setting.
> Procedures such as but not limited to:
> Cryotherapy of warts, superficial skin lesions, molluscum contagiosum
> No follow up needed unless warts need additional treatments, typically about 2-3 weeks after cryotherapy session.
> Shave excision with electrocautery of assumed benign superficial skin lesions
> Skin tags, inflamed seborrheic keratoses, etc.
> Typically, no follow up needed unless patient sees signs of infection or delayed healing.
> Punch biopsy for full thickness assessment of lesions
> Typically follow up for suture removal- timing based on location of lesion removal
> 
> Full resection of skin lesions, cysts, lipomas
> Same as punch biopsy, follow up for suture removal and to check on healing status.
> Sooner follow up if signs of infection develop
> Suturing of skin lacerations
> Follow up for suture removal, timing based on location of injury
> I&D of abscesses with packing as needed
> Follow up timing depends on size and amount of packing material required.
