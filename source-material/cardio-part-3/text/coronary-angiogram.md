## Slide 3

- Cardiac Catheterization
- Gold standard in diagnosis of coronary atherosclerosis

## Slide 4

- Coronary Angiogram Indications
- Angina that significantly interferes with a patients lifestyle despite maximal tolerable medical therapy
- Patients with high risk criteria on noninvasive testing
- Atypical symptoms with equivocal findings on stress testing in whom a diagnosis needs to be established
- New onset cardiomyopathy

## Slide 5

- Coronary Angiogram Indications
- Initial test:
  - Following sudden cardiac arrest
  - Life threatening ventricular arrhythmia
  - New onset symptomatic heart failure
  - ACS (STEMI, NSTEMI, unstable angina)

## Slide 6

- Contraindications (relative)
- Acute renal failure
- Active gastrointestinal bleeding
- Unexplained fever, which may be due to infection
- Untreated active infection
- Acute stroke
- Severe anemia
- Severe uncontrolled HTN
- Severe symptomatic electrolyte imbalance
- Severe systemic illness
- Severe concomitant illness that drastically shortens life
- Refusal of patient to consider definitive therapy such as PCI
- Digitalis intoxication
- Documented anaphylactic reaction to contrast dye
- Severe peripheral artery disease limiting vascular access
- Decompensated heart failure or acute pulmonary edema
- Severe coagulopathy
- Aortic valve endocarditis

## Slide 7

- Heart Catheterization Pre-op
- ACE-I, ARB, and diuretics may be held the morning of the procedure
- Anticoagulation is held prior to procedure. INR must be < 1.5
- Metformin is held the day of and 48 hours following procedure
- Nothing to eat or drink after midnight the night before
- Patient is shaved and given a sedative to relax

## Slide 16

- Sheath Removal
- Sheath remains in place until anticoagulants are below their peak activation (Usually 4 hrs following the last dose of heparin)
- Hourly vital signs
- 30 minute groin checks
- Sheath is remove and pressure is applied to femoral artery simultaneously
- Pressure is applied for approximately 20 mins or until hemostasis is obtained
- Bedrest for 6 hours following

## Slide 17

- Post-op
- Vital signs:
  - Every 15 mins for 1 hr
  - Every 30 mins for 3 hrs
  - Every 4 hours for 24 hrs
- No heavy lifting (>10 lbs) for 1 week
- No strenuous exercise for 1 week

## Slide 22

- Complications
- Death (0.1%)
- MI (0.1%)
- Stroke (0.4% during PCI)
- Vascular complications:
  - Acute thrombosis/distal embolization
  - Dissection (acute)
  - Hematoma/hemorrhage (acute)
  - Pseudoaneurysm (late)
  - AV fistula (late)
  - Retroperitoneal hemorrhage
- Arrhythmia
- Allergic reaction (iodine contrast agent)
- Acute kidney injury

## Slide 23

- Allergy To Contrast Dye
- Prednisone 50 mg 13, 7, and 1 hour prior to procedure
- Benadryl 50 mg 1 hour prior to procedure

## Slide 24

- Right Heart Cath
- Pulmonay artery catheters (Swan-Ganz catheters)
- Direct measurements:
  - Central venous pressure (CVP)
  - Right-sided intracardiac pressures
  - Pulmonary arterial pressure (Pap)
  - Pulmonary capillary occlusion pressure (PCOP)
  - Cardiac output (CO)
  - Mixed venous oxyhemoglobin saturation (SvO2)

## Slide 25

- Indications
- Unexplained or unknown volume status in shock
- Severe cardiogenic shock
- Suspected or known pulmonary artery hypertension
- Severe underlying cardiopulmonary disease (congenital heart disease, left-to-right shunt, severe valvular disease, pulmonary hypertension)

## Slide 27

- Coronary CT Angiography
- Non-invasive coronary angiography
- Indicated for patients who have intermediate risk factors with equivocal stress test results
- Useful in patients with known or suspected congenital coronary anomalies.
- Patient is typically given BB to slow down heart rate to 60-70 bpm, nitroglycerin for vasodilation and IV contrast dye

> Notes: Or non-invasive visualization of the coronary arteries

## Slide 28

- Coronary CT Angiogram
- Negative predictive value of 95-100% for significant CAD in multicenter studies
- Positive predictive value 91-93% for left main disease and bypass grafts
- Positive predictive value of 60-84% for other coronary arteries

## Slide 29

- Limitations
- Heart rate greater than 70 bpm
- Irregular heart rhythm
- Inability to hold breath for five seconds
- Severe coronary calcification or prior stents

## Slide 30

- Coronary Calcium Scoring
- Screening tool for asymptomatic patients
- Recommended for low-intermediate and intermediate risk patients
- Predicts the extent of coronary atherosclerosis or plaque burden
- CAN NOT measure severity of coronary stenosis
- Highly predictive of the absence of coronary atherosclerosis

## Slide 31

- Coronary Calcium Scoring
- 0 – no identifiable disease
- 1 to 99 – mild disease
- 100 to 399 – moderate disease
- >400 – severe disease
