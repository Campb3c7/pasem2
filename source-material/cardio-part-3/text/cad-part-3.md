## Slide 2

- Chest Pain
- Chest pain is the 2nd most common reason for adults to present to the ED in the United States
- Chest pain also leads to nearly 4 million outpatient visits annually in the United States
- Although the cause of chest pain is often noncardiac, CAD affects >18.2 million adults in the United States and remains the leading cause of death for men and women, accounting for >365 000 deaths annually
- Distinguishing between serious and benign causes of chest pain is imperative

## Slide 3

- Angina Pectoris
- Angina pectoris is chest pain or discomfort caused by myocardial ischemia
- Patients describe retrosternal pressure, tightness, or heaviness—not always "pain"—with radiation to arm, jaw, shoulder, or back
- Associated symptoms include dyspnea, diaphoresis, and nausea
- "Silent" ischemia can occur in elderly and diabetic patients

## Slide 4

- Angina Pectoris
- Stable angina: Triggered by exertion or emotional stress; resolves with rest; caused by fixed atherosclerotic obstruction with subendocardial ischemia
- Unstable angina: Occurs at rest or with minimal exertion; indicates plaque rupture with thrombosis; medical emergency
- Vasospastic (Prinzmetal) angina: Caused by coronary artery spasm; can occur at rest; causes transmural ischemia with ST-segment elevation

- Symptoms and Signs of Cardiac Ischemia
- Other symptoms on history that may suggest coronary artery disease:
  - Progressively worsening dyspnea or exercise intolerance
  - Palpitations, lightheadedness
  - Weight gain (days) or lower extremity edema

## Slide 11

- Chest Pain Classification
- 2021 AHA/ACC Chest Pain Guideline:
- Cardiac - Chest pain occurring because of an underlying cardiac etiology. Includes classic chest discomfort based on quality, location, radiation, and provoking and relieving factors that makes it more likely to be of cardiac ischemic origin.
- Possible Cardiac - Chest pain symptoms that suggest a cardiac origin
- Noncardiac - Chest pain symptoms likely because of a noncardiac cause in patients with persistent or recurring symptoms despite a negative stress test or anatomic cardiac evaluation, or a low-risk designation by a clinical decision pathway

## Slide 22

- What Are the Clinical Syndromes of Symptomatic CAD?
- There are four disease manifestations which may occur due to the process of coronary atherosclerosis - numbers 2, 3 and 4 are considered acute coronary syndromes (ACS).
    - Stable angina
    - Unstable angina (UA)
    - Non-ST elevation MI or NSTEMI
    - ST-segment elevation MI or STEMI

## Slide 23

- Symptomatic CAD: Stable Angina
- Stable angina: is characterized by predictable and reproducible symptoms of cardiac ischemia induced by exercise/exertion, cold exposure, or emotional stress.
- Symptoms typically last 5-10 minutes and are relieved by rest or nitroglycerin.
- Stable angina most commonly results from a fixed, flow-limiting atherosclerotic plaque that limits coronary blood flow during periods of increased myocardial oxygen demand.

## Slide 26

- Acute Coronary Syndromes
- The acute coronary syndromes include:
    - Unstable angina
    - Non-ST elevation MI or NSTEMI
    - ST elevation MI or STEMI
- ACS may be caused by:
  - Sudden atherosclerotic plaque rupture.
  - Growth of the plaque to a critical level.
  - Stress from a non-cardiac illness that taxes the diseased heart’s ability to cope.

## Slide 27

- Acute Coronary Syndromes
- Acute coronary syndromes are marked by an increase in the symptoms of cardiac ischemia in terms of:
  - Frequency
  - Severity
  - Duration
  - Provocation - occurring with measurably less exertion or possibly occurring at rest.
  - Relief - the symptoms are less responsive to rest or SL nitroglycerin.

## Slide 44

- Troponin I and T
- Troponin is a protein found in muscle cells that contains three subunits: T, C, and I
- Troponin is spilled into the circulation from damaged cardiac myocytes during myocardial ischemia.  It can also be “leaked” when there is significant cardiac stress.  For example- tachycardic dysrhythmias, RV failure from a PE, and decompensated HF

## Slide 45

- Troponin I
- Troponin I is found only in cardiac cells and is therefore the most specific to cardiac muscle damage.
- Troponin I becomes detectable in the serum between 4 -6 hours after onset of cardiac ischemia.
- Troponin peaks and then falls to lower levels and remains elevated at these low levels for 7-10 days after an event if checked in a regular sensitivity assay.

## Slide 46

- Troponin I
- Troponin is both sensitive and specific for MI in the right clinical circumstances
  - Sensitivity: 50% at 4 hours, 97% at 6 hours
  - Specificity: 95%
- Troponin is chronically elevated in ESRD and CKD stage IV because of impaired clearance.  Look for a prior level to determine if the patient is above their usual baseline.
- An initial troponin can be negative/ normal, if the CP patient presented to the ER quickly.

## Slide 47

- High Sensitivity Troponin
- In 2021, new guidelines were published by the ACC recommending the use of high sensitivity troponin for the evaluation of chest pain.
- Both high-sensitivity and traditional troponin assays measure the exact same molecule, but HS assays measure much more precisely and at much lower concentrations.
  - With traditional troponin assays, a troponin level was not detectable in most patients who lacked cardiac ischemia.  To qualify as a high-sensitivity troponin assay, the test must be able to detect a level of cardiac troponin in >50% of normal individuals. This means that the majority of patients without cardiac ischemia will have a detectable level of cardiac troponin.

## Slide 48

- High Sensitivity Troponin
- High-sensitivity troponin assays have significantly higher sensitivity for ACS than conventional troponin assays.  This means that they have a much higher negative predictive value.
- In other words no elevation of troponin, no acute MI
- This allows clinicians to more quickly and accurately identify those CP patients who do not have ACS and who are at very low risk of 30-day MACE (major adverse cardiac events).

## Slide 49

- High Sensitivity Troponin
- However, the cost of the increased sensitivity is a decreased specificity, yielding a lower positive predictive value.
- This means that more people with a non-cardiac cause for their symptoms, who happen to have a detectable troponin, will be evaluated for ACS/ CAD.
  - Remember that HS troponin is detectable in > 50% of asymptomatic individuals who are at their baseline health.

## Slide 50

- High Sensitivity Troponin
- Therefore, HS assays expedite the evaluation of suspected ACS patients and determine who is low-risk and therefore able to discharge to home.
  - There is good data supporting the use of a single HS troponin value in a patient presenting > 2 hours after symptom onset.  If the troponin is less than the sex-specific 99th percentile, this substantially lessens the concern for ACS.
  - We also evaluate the delta (change) between 2 serial HS troponin levels, taken 90-120 minutes apart.  If the patient has 2 levels below the sex-specific 99th percentile and minimal change  discharge to home.

## Slide 51

- Brain Natriuretic Peptide (BNP)
- Brain natriuretic peptide (BNP) is a natriuretic hormone initially identified in the brain but released primarily from the cardiac ventricles.
- The final cleavage of the prohormone produces biologically active BNP as well as biologically inert N-terminal pro-BNP (NT-proBNP).
- Facilities usually choose to measure one.  Levels of BNP and NT-pro-BNP are not comparable.

## Slide 52

- Brain Natriuretic Peptide (BNP)
- The release of BNP is increased in decompensated HF, as cells secrete BNP in response to the high ventricular filling pressures to promote sodium wasting in the urine
- BNP has a diuretic and natriuretic effect, inhibits the RAAS and sympathetic activity and has a positive inotropic effect on the ventricles.
- BNP is renally cleared and thus NOT HELPFUL in AKI or significant CKD.  Always elevated.

## Slide 54

- Initial Evaluation of the CP PatientPutting It Together
- Step 2: Obtain a 12-lead EKG – within 10 minutes
- Obtain and interpret the EKG within 10 minutes of arrival or first medical contact.
- Look for ST-segment elevation or other findings concerning for acute ischemia.
- If the initial EKG is nondiagnostic but suspicion remains, obtain serial EKGs.
- Do not delay emergency reperfusion decisions while waiting for troponin results when the EKG and clinical presentation indicate an acute coronary occlusion.

## Slide 55

- Initial Evaluation of the CP PatientPutting It Together
- Step 3: Obtain initial labs and begin treatment
- Obtain high-sensitivity cardiac troponin (hs-cTn).
- Consider CBC, electrolytes/renal function, glucose, and other tests as indicated.
- Give chewable ASA 325mg, when ACS is suspected and there is no contraindication.

## Slide 69

- The HEART Pathway is a clinical decision pathway that combines the HEART score with a nonischemic EKG and serial negative troponins to identify patients who may be appropriate for early discharge.
- HEART score
  - History · EKG · Age · Risk factors · Troponin
  - A score of 0–3 indicates a lower-risk category.
- Confirm pathway criteria
  - No ischemic EKG changes
  - Negative serial troponins
  - HEART score ≤3
- Low-risk definition: Estimated 30-day risk of death or MACE below 1%, using a validated clinical decision pathway
- IP Evaluation of the Chest Pain Patient

## Slide 73

- CCTA
- Noninvasive imaging test that uses CT and IV contrast to visualize the coronary arteries.
- Commonly used in patients with stable chest pain and an intermediate-to-high likelihood of CAD who have no known CAD.
- Identifies coronary atherosclerotic plaque and evaluates the degree of coronary artery narrowing (stenosis).

## Slide 74

- CCTA
- Advantages
  - Visualizes coronary artery anatomy and plaque.
  - Can identify both calcified and noncalcified plaque.
  - A normal study (no plaque or stenosis) makes obstructive CAD very unlikely.
- Limitations
  - Requires IV iodinated contrast.
  - Image quality may be limited by extensive coronary calcification, irregular/rapid heart rate, or inability to adequately control heart rate.
  - Does not directly determine whether a coronary lesion causes myocardial ischemia.

## Slide 75

- Stress Test
- When is stress testing used?
- Used to evaluate for inducible myocardial ischemia in clinically stable patients with suspected or known CAD.
- Increases myocardial oxygen demand or alters coronary blood flow to determine whether coronary perfusion is adequate.
- Two approaches to stress testing:
- Exercise stress testing
- Pharmacologic stress testing
- Stress testing may be performed with or without imaging:
- Exercise ECG alone
- Stress echocardiography
- Nuclear myocardial perfusion imaging (MPI)
- Stress cardiac MRI

## Slide 76

## Slide 86

- LHC Findings: Interpreting Coronary Anatomy
- Angiography estimates how much the coronary artery lumen is narrowed.
- ≥70%
- Non-left-main coronary artery stenosis is generally considered anatomically significant.
- ≥50%
- Left main coronary artery stenosis is considered significant.
- INTERMEDIATE STENOSES
- For selected lesions, FFR ≤0.80 or iFR ≤0.89 indicates physiologic significance.

## Slide 87

- LHC Findings: TIMI flow
| Grade | Description | Clinical meaning |
| 0 | No flow | Complete occlusion |
| 1 | Penetration without perfusion | Minimal flow beyond obstruction |
| 2 | Partial perfusion | Distal artery fills, but flow is delayed |
| 3 | Normal perfusion | Normal coronary flow |
- TIMI flow is a grading system used during coronary angiography to describe how well blood flows through a coronary artery, particularly after an intervention such as PCI
- Clinical takeaway
- After successful PCI, the goal is restoration of TIMI 3 flow.

## Slide 88

- LHC Findings: Fractional Flow Reserve (FFR)
- FFR measures the pressure beyond a coronary artery stenosis compared with the pressure before the stenosis.
- This allows the cardiologist to determine whether stenosis is restricting blood flow enough to cause myocardial ischemia.

## Slide 90

- Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI)
- How PCI restores coronary blood flow:
- Balloon angioplasty: A catheter-mounted balloon is positioned across the stenosis and inflated to expand the narrowed segment.
- Stent placement: A metal mesh stent is expanded to help maintain vessel patency.
- Drug-eluting stent (DES): Most commonly used; releases medication that reduces neointimal growth and restenosis.
- Bare-metal stent (BMS): A metal scaffold without medication that helps keep the artery open but has a higher risk of restenosis due to neointimal growth.

## Slide 94

- Thrombosis vs Restenosis
- In-stent thrombosis (Sudden event)
- Acute clot formation within the stent.
- Can occur hours to years after PCI.
- Often presents as ACS or acute MI.
- Risk factors include premature DAPT discontinuation, inadequate stent expansion, and malapposition.
- In-stent restenosis (Usually gradual process)
- Recurrent narrowing within the stented segment.
- Primarily caused by neointimal hyperplasia; may also involve neoatherosclerosis.
- May cause recurrent angina or ischemia.
- Thrombosis = clot formation.
- Restenosis = tissue growth and recurrent narrowing.

## Slide 95

- Intracoronary Stents & Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT)
- Stent placement activates platelets: Balloon expansion and stent deployment injure the vessel lining and expose thrombogenic surfaces.
- Risk of stent thrombosis: Platelets can aggregate on the stent before it becomes covered by healthy endothelial cells.
- Preventing thrombosis: Dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) combines:
        - Aspirin – inhibits platelet thromboxane A₂ production.
        - P2Y12 inhibitor – blocks ADP-mediated platelet activation.
- Common P2Y12 inhibitors:
      - Thienopyridines: clopidogrel, prasugrel
      - Non-thienopyridine: ticagrelor

## Slide 96

- Duration of Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT) After PCI
- ACS treated with PCI - 12 months
- Aspirin + an oral P2Y12 inhibitor for at least 12 months in patients who are not at high bleeding risk.
- Examples: STEMI, NSTEMI, and unstable angina treated with PCI.
- Chronic coronary disease treated with PCI - 6 months
- Aspirin + clopidogrel for 6 months, followed by single antiplatelet therapy

## Slide 97

- CTO- Chronic Total Occlusions
- A CTO is a 100% coronary artery occlusion with TIMI 0 flow that has been present for ≥3 months.
- As coronary obstruction develops gradually, collateral vessels may enlarge to provide an alternative blood supply to the myocardium.
- Collateral circulation may maintain blood flow at rest but may be insufficient to meet increased oxygen demand during exercise.
- Some patients remain asymptomatic, while others develop exertional angina or myocardial ischemia.

## Slide 98

- CTO- Chronic Total Occlusions
- Treatment options include:
- Optimal medical therapy: Antianginal medications and aggressive cardiovascular risk reduction.
- CTO PCI: Specialized techniques allow interventional cardiologists to cross and reopen the occluded artery, followed by balloon angioplasty and usually DES placement.
- CABG: Considered in selected patients based on coronary anatomy, symptoms, and overall disease burden.
- When is CTO PCI considered?
- Persistent, lifestyle-limiting angina despite optimal medical therapy.
- Evidence of significant ischemia or viable myocardium in the territory supplied by the occluded artery.

## Slide 113

- Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting
- The saphenous vein, internal mammary artery, or radial artery are harvested and anastomosed to ascending aorta and coronary artery to BYPASS areas of atherosclerosis.
- Sometimes, a patient with CAD is better served by a coronary artery bypass grafting procedure than by a stent.

## Slide 114

- Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting
- When is CABG preferred over PCI?
- Significant left main disease: stenosis ≥50%.
- Multivessel CAD with LV dysfunction:
  - EF less than 35%: CABG recommended in appropriate patients.
  - EF 35–50%: CABG reasonable in selected patients.
- Diabetes with multivessel CAD involving the LAD: CABG preferred over PCI in appropriate surgical candidates.
- Complex or diffuse multivessel disease: CABG may offer better long-term outcomes.

## Slide 117
