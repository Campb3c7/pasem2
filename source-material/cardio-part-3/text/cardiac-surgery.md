## Slide 19

- Interventions
- When medical management fails:
- PCI is favored to CABG in patients with one or two vessel disease not involving the proximal left anterior descending coronary artery.
- CABG is favored to PCI in patients with three vessel disease and SYNTAX score ≥23; in patients with lower SYNTAX scores, PCI is considered a reasonable alternative.
- CABG is favored to PCI in patients with Left Main CAD, again except for patients with Syntax Score <22.

## Slide 26

- Cardio Pulmonary Bypass (CPB)
- Extracorporeal circulation
- Blood is anticoagulated
- All blood is removed from the body, usually from Right Atrium
- It is Oxygenated, filtered, temperature controlled, and returned to circulation, usually via Aorta, BUT flow is non pulsatile!
- Heart is stopped by cold Cardioplegia solution, high in Potassium
- Target flow rate is 2.2 to 2.4 liters/minute with a  pressure of 65-100 mm Hg.
- At the end of the procedure the cardioplegia is removed, the heart is filled with blood, restarted, and patient is taken off bypass.

## Slide 27

- Why is the patient made hypothermic?
- At normothermia (37°C), the flow required is approximately 2.4 L/min/m2, but with hypothermia, oxygen consumption is reduced by 50% for every 10°C drop in temperature, and a flow of only 1L/min/m2 is required at 18°C
- The heart is stopped using cardioplegia, a potassium rich solution usually mixed with autologous blood.

> Notes: Retrograde cardioplegia has the advantage of more uniform distribution in patients with diffuse coronary artery disease and is not dependent on a competent aortic valve for delivery.

## Slide 31

- Graft Choices
- Vein Grafts (Saphenous)
  - Most common
  - Patency <90% at 1 year is common
  - High failure rate – 10-25% in 12-18 months
  - Early occlusion (in 30 Days) in 10% of SVG
  - Late occlusion
    - Year 1-6 2%/year
    - Year 7 on 4-5%/year
  - Dependent on runoff/size of native artery

## Slide 51

- Post-op Monitoring
- Chest/Mediastinal tube outputs – Are they bleeding?
- Urine output/Vital Signs – Are the kidneys being perfused?  Are there indications of hypovolemia?
- Monitor electrolytes, ABG, Hgb concentration, Platelets, BUN, Serum Creatinine Daily – Are the Kidneys working?  Are they developing Anemia?  Are they acidotic, are they perfusing?  Are they oxygenating?
- Blood sugar q 6 – Are they Hypo or Hyperglycemic?
- Echocardiography – Visual evidence of cardiac function
- ECG – Ischemic Changes?
- CXR – Pulmonary edema?  Hypervolemia?  Pneumonia?
