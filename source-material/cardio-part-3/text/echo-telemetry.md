## Slide 5

- Transthoracic Echocardiogram (TTE)
- Primary noninvasive imaging modality for quantitative and qualitative evaluation of cardiac anatomy and function
- Structural components of the heart
  - Biventricular function (left ventricular systolic function, diastolic function, right ventricular function)
  - Structure abnormalities (left ventricular hypertrophy, wall motion abnormalities, septal aneurysm, PFO, ASD)
  - Four heart valves; aortic, mitral, tricuspid, pulmonic

## Slide 7

- Parasternal views
- Long axis – useful to estimate the size and contractility of the right and left ventricle (septum and posterior wall), to assess the morphology and function of the mitral and aortic valves. With color Doppler you can look for an aortic or mitral regurgitation and try to determine their mechanism.
- Short axis - There are 3 levels of imaging the heart in short axis:
  - Base of the heart, level of the aortic valve.
  - Level of the mitral valve
  - Mid-ventricle: papillary muscles and toward the apex
- [picture]

## Slide 8

- Apical views
- 4 chamber view - In this view you will be able to visualize the 4 main chambers of the heart: left ventricle and atrium, right ventricle and atrium. The Apical 4 chamber view is the best view to calculate the Simpson ejection fraction, to visualize the apex of the left ventricle, and to study the mitral inflow (diastolic function and mitral stenosis)
- 5 chamber view - The apical 5 chamber view differs from the apical 4 chamber view by the presence of the aortic valve in the center of the image. This is the view where you will get the best alignement of your Doppler beam with the aortic outflow tract, to look and grade an aortic stenosis and to calculate the cardiac output.
- 2 and 3 chamber - The apical 2 chamber view will show you the anterior and inferior walls of the left ventricle.
- [picture]

> Notes: 5 chamber view best for visualization of aortic outflow tract and measuring aortic stenosis

## Slide 9

- Subcostal Views
- 4 chamber - In some ventilated patients, the subcostal view will be the only window accessible. You will be able to assess LV and RV function and valves regurgitations
- Short axis - From a subcostal approach, it is possible to obtain a short axis of the left ventricle, to estimate LV function, and a short axis of the aortic valve with visualization of the tricuspid and pulmonic valves.
- Inferior vena cava - Determination of the IVC diameter and respiratory variations are pivotal for the estimation of a patient's volume status.

## Slide 16

- TEE Indications
- Suspected acute aortic pathology (ie, dissection, transsection, intramural hematoma)
- Suspected prosthetic valve dysfunction (thrombus, pannus ingrowth, vegetation, or regurgitation)
- Suspected complications of endocarditis (eg, fistula, abscess)
- Evaluation for left atrial/LAA thrombus in a patient with atrial fibrillation/atrial flutter to facilitate clinical decision making regarding anticoagulation, cardioversion, or ablation
- To diagnose infective endocarditis in patients with moderate or high pretest likelihood (ie, patients with prosthetic valves, certain pathogens)
- To evaluate for cardiac source of embolus
- As follow-up to prior TEE when an interval change would result in a change in therapy (eg, resolution of vegetation following antimicrobial therapy)
- To evaluate for cardiac pathology when transthoracic echocardiography is nondiagnostic
- To facilitate clinical decision making regarding anticoagulation, cardioversion, or ablation in patients with atrial fibrillation/flutter To provide guidance for noncoronary percutaneous cardiac interventions (eg, placement of closure devices, valvuloplasty, percutaneous valves)

## Slide 18

- Contraindications (relative)

- Thrombocytopenia
- Zenker’s diverticulum
- Altered mental status
- Severe cervical spine arthritis (reduce range of motion leads to increased risk of complications)

## Slide 19

- Complications
- Esophageal perforation
- Gastrointestinal bleeding – An increased risk of bleeding should be considered among patients with thrombocytopenia (platelet count <50 k),
- Pharyngeal hematoma
- Methemoglobinemia – Methemoglobinemia is a rare but potentially life-threatening complication of topical benzocaine and related agents used for posterior pharyngeal anesthesia
- Minor complications:
  - Transient bronchospasm
  - Transient hypoxia
  - NSVT
  - AF
  - Hemoptysis
  - Vomiting

## Slide 20

- TEE vs TTE
- Transthoracic echo remains the initial test of choice for most patients requiring an echocardiogram. However, a transesophageal echo should be performed as the initial test in certain life-threatening situations or in situations where transthoracic echo is likely to be non-diagnostic
| TEE | TTE |
| Superior visualization of posterior structures (best view of mitral valve) | Quick |
| Minimally invasive | Non-invasive |
| Requires skilled physician | Performed by echo tech |

## Slide 21

- Bubble Study
- Contrast echo can be useful for confirming a diagnosis of atrial septal defect (ASD) or patent foramen ovale (PFO). Agitating saline or synthetic contrast create microbubbles. These are very reflective, and when injected intravenously can be seen as opacification in the echo window. They are normally seen on the right side of the heart before being trapped and absorbed by the pulmonary capillaries, so have no route to the left side of the heart. The contrast created by the bubbles allows a left-to-right shunt to be seen as a jet "interrupting" the opacification of the right atrium. However, there is a theoretical risk of systemic air embolism with a right-to-left shunt.

## Slide 23

- MUGA scan
- Preferred technique for measurement of the left ventricular ejection fraction with high degree of accuracy
- Very accurate and reproducible
- Body habitus does not limit technique
- Non-invasive
- Radiation exposure – 620 mrem
- Contraindicated in pregnancy or lactating women

> Notes: CXR – 10 mrem, CT chest – 700 mrem, CT head – 200 mrem

## Slide 25

- Cardiac Telemetry
- Continuous monitoring of the heart’s electrical activity
- View of two or three leads of an ECG
- Indicated for:
  - Suspected arrhythmia
  - Unexplained syncope
  - Evaluation of therapeutic intervention

## Slide 27

- 30 Day Event Monitor
- Monitor patient’s rhythm for 2-4 weeks
- Used for high index of suspicion for arrhythmia, detect afib burden, etc…
- Mailed to patient’s house with instructions and later returned to office
- Patient is allowed to remove event monitor to shower
- Uncomfortable/patient compliance
