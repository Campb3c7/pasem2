PHAS 517 Cardiology CMM Instructional Objectives
By the end of the cardiovascular modules, the first-year PA student will be able to:

Cardiology Anatomy Objectives:

Thorax:

Describe the thoracic cage and parts that make up the sternum.
Identify the important landmarks of the manubrium, body, and xiphoid process of the sternum.
Explain the anatomical surface landmarks and lines of the thorax.
Mediastinum:

Describe the components of the superior and inferior mediastinum.
Describe the layers of the pericardium.
Heart:

Discuss the external surface landmarks of the heart.
Describe the anatomical structure of the heart including:  the four chambers, internal fibrous skeleton, valves, myoelectric conducting system, coronary arteries, and veins.
Differentiate the surface projection of a valve location from the auscultation point on the chest wall for heart sounds.
Blood vessels of the Thorax. Abdomen & Extremities

Describe the location of major arteries and veins of the thorax.
Identify major arteries and veins of the abdomen.
Identify major arteries and veins (superficial and deep) of the extremities.
Cardiology Physiology Objectives:

Describe the organization of the circulatory system (systemic & pulmonic).
Explain the function of the systemic & pulmonic circulatory systems.
Discuss the principles of blood flow and how they are related to one another.
Discuss the cardiac conduction system and how it works, including “absolute & relative refractory periods" as well as the supernormal excitatory period.
Describe the phases of the cardiac cycle including systole & diastole and their subcomponents.
Discuss how the following four factors affect cardiac output (preload, afterload, cardiac contractility, and heart rate).
Compare and contrast the arterial and venous circulatory systems and functions.
Describe autoregulation, endothelial control, and humoral control in regards to blood flow.
Cardiovascular H&P Objectives:

Recall the historical questions associated with angina, palpitations, shortness of breath, edema, peripheral vascular symptoms, syncope, and abdominal/back/flank pain.  
Determine when to use chronic care questions for chronic diseases and which questions are necessary to evaluate chronic diseases.
Identify the anatomical landmarks used to perform an examination of the cardiovascular system.
Associate the anatomical cardiac valves with the appropriate auscultation points.
Compare and contrast normal versus abnormal skin findings related to the cardiovascular system.
Describe the method for locating the PMI, determine its associated anatomical portion of the heart, and identify etiologies of an abnormal PMI.
Compare and contrast normal versus abnormal inspection and palpation of the chest wall.
Differentiate between the diaphragm and bell in regard to the best detection of heart sounds.
Differentiate between S1 and S2 based on the pathophysiology and auscultation of the heart.  Describe the physiological split of S2 and how to augment it.
Describe S3 and S4 heart sounds and recall their association with diagnoses.
Describe techniques to augment mitral stenosis and aortic regurgitation.
Describe the technique to estimate jugular venous pressure and determine the clinical significance of its measurement.  Recall normal JVP.
Associate arteries with their palpable locations, describe the best method to palpate pulses, determine grading of pulses, and describe the best method to auscultate bruits.
Describe the best techniques for each of the special tests associated with the cardiovascular system and interpret results of the special tests.
Discuss the cultural considerations related to the cardiovascular system.
Cardiovascular OSCE Objectives:

Integrate and apply knowledge of specific cardiovascular diseases to a clinical scenario.
Construct a thorough history of present illness for a patient with a chief complaint related to the cardiovascular system.

Develop a list of differential diagnoses based on the information collected during the history and physical exam.
Order and interpret appropriate diagnostic tests related to the cardiovascular system.
Draft appropriate medical documentation of the patient encounter to include an HPI, physical exam findings, medical decision-making, treatment plan, patient education, and follow-up.
Utilize professional interpersonal communication skills.
Integrate knowledge from previous course presentations and labs.
EKG Objectives:

Define the key properties of myocardial cells.
Explain the heart’s conduction pathway and point out the depolarization and repolarization portions of the atria and ventricles on the EKG.
Recall the normal appearance, amplitude, and duration of the P wave, PR segment, PR interval, QRS complex, J point, ST segment, QT interval, and T wave.
Recall when U waves and delta waves appear on an EKG.
Compare and contrast physiologic and pathologic Q waves.
Compare and contrast the views of the limb and precordial leads on a 12 lead EKG.
Interpret rhythm strips and 12 lead EKGs using a stepwise approach.
Explain the pathophysiology and distinguish the appearance of normal sinus rhythm, sinus bradycardia, sinus tachycardia, sinus dysrhythmia/arrhythmia, sinus arrest/pause, atrial tachycardia, wandering atrial pacemaker, multifocal atrial tachycardia, atrial flutter, atrial fibrillation, junctional bradycardia, junctional rhythm, accelerated junctional rhythm, junctional tachycardia, idioventricular rhythm, accelerated idioventricular rhythm, ventricular tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation, asystole, and AV blocks (1st degree, 2nd degree types I and II, 3rd degree) on an EKG.
Recognize PACs, PJCs, and PVCs on an EKG.
Explain the concerning arrhythmias that can arise from a prolonged QTc interval.
Identify the correct axis on an EKG and recall the conditions that can cause an altered electrical axis.
Explain the pathophysiology of and identify hypertrophy, enlargement, ventricular strain, bundle branch blocks, and hemiblocks on an EKG.
Identify pericarditis, pericardial effusion, pulmonary embolus, and pacemakers on an EKG.
Differentiate myocardial damage on an EKG and identify what area of the heart is damaged on a 12 lead EKG.
Determine diagnostic ST segment elevation on a 12 lead EKG.
Recognize changes on an EKG caused by hyperkalemia, hypokalemia, hypercalcemia, and hypocalcemia.
