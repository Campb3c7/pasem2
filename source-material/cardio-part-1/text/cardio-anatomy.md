## Slide 18

- Pericardium
- Composed of fibrous and serous layers
- Fibrous layer blends with fibrous layer of roots of great vessels and central tendon of diaphragm for stability
- Serous layer produces pericardial fluid (~ 30 – 50 mL) to allow smooth contractions of the heart within the pericardial cavity (a potential space); two portions – parietal and visceral
  - Parietal is lining of fibrous pericardium
  - Visceral is the epicardium covering the heart

## Slide 39

- The Heart
- Thick connective tissue forms 4 connecting rings for heart valve attachment
- Fibrous skeleton:
  - Separates atria from ventricles
  - Keeps orifices of the AV and semilunar valves patent and prevents them from being overly distended by increased volume of blood pumping through
  - Provides attachment for the leaflets and cusps of the valves
  - Provides attachment for the myocardial muscles
  - Forms an electrical “insulator” by separating the conducted impulses of the atria and ventricles so that they conduct independently

> Notes: Diseased valves become stiff or leaky

## Slide 49

- Cardiac Circulation
- Coronary Arteries – first branches off aorta; supply epicardium and myocardium; much variation in distribution.
- Right coronary (RCA) – arises from right aortic sinus passing to the right side of the pulmonary trunk in the coronary sulcus; branches include –
- Right marginal – supplies rt. border of heart toward apex (doesn’t reach it)
- SA nodal – supplies SA node (60% of people)
- AV nodal – supplies AV node (80% of people)
- Posterior (Descending) Interventricular (PIV) – supplies posterior adjacent areas of both ventricles
- Interventricular septal – penetrating branch of PIV; supplies posterior 1/3rd of septum and part of left ventricle

> Notes: Supplies the right atrium and most of rt ventricle

## Slide 50

- Cardiac Circulation (cont)
- Coronary Arteries (cont)
- Left coronary artery (LCA) – arises from left aortic sinus of aorta; passes between left auricle and left side of pulmonary trunk and continues in the coronary sulcus; its branches are as follows:
- Left anterior (IV) descending – supplies anterior parts of both ventricles, anterior 2/3 of IVS,  the apex, & many times anastomoses with Rt. PIV
- Lateral branch – supplies anterior surface of heart
- Circumflex – follows sulcus around left border to posterior surface of heart supplying the left atrium, mitral valve, and most of the posterior left ventricle
- Left marginal – supplies left ventricle

## Slide 55

- Electrical Conducting System
- Sinoatrial (SA) node
  - The pacemaker of heart
  - Initiates and regulates the impulses for contractions
  - Located anterolaterally just deep to the epicardium at the junction of the SVC & Rt. Atrium
  - Stimulated by sympathetic autonomic nervous system (ANS) & inhibited by the parasympathetic ANS
- Atrioventricular (AV) node
  - Smaller than SA node
  - Located in posteroinferior region of interatrial septum near opening of coronary sinus
  - The signal generated by the SA node passes through the walls of the right atrium to the AV node
  - Which then distribute the signal to the ventricles through the AV Bundle

## Slide 56

- Electrical Conducting System
- AV Bundle (Bundle of His)
  - Only electrical connection between atria and ventricles
  - Passes through fibrous skeleton of heart and along the membranous part of IVS
  - At the junction of the membranous and muscular parts of IVS, the AV bundle divides into the right and left bundles
- Right & left bundle branches
  - Right branch supplies right ventricle, left branch supplies left ventricle
  - These branches proceed on each side of muscular IVS and then become the Purkinje fibers
- Subendocardial branches (Purkinje fibers)
  - Extend into the walls of each respective ventricle and stimulate the muscles along with the IVS.
