## Slide 2

- Definition
- Hypertensive urgency: systolic BP ≥ 180 and/or diastolic BP ≥ 120 mmHg
  - no acute or progressing target-organ injury
- Hypertensive emergency (AKA Hypertensive Crisis): systolic BP ≥ 180 and/or diastolic BP ≥ 120 mmHg
  - acute or progressing target-organ damage
    - CNS: CVA (stroke ischemic or hemorrhagic), encephalopathy,
    - CV: acute LV failure with pulmonary edema, MI, aortic dissection, unstable angina, eclampsia
    - Renal: AKI

## Slide 3

- Causes of Hypertensive crisis
- Non-adherence to antihypertensive med (MC)
- Undiagnosed Hypertension
- Abrupt cessation of antihypertensive med- BB or clonidine
- CKD
- OSA
- Drugs- Cocaine amphetamines, diet pills, phenylephrine

## Slide 4

- Pathophysiology of Hypertensive Emergencies
- The cause and events of a hypertensive crisis are incompletely understood.
- Failure of normal autoregulation
  - Autoregulation at the level of each organ helps to prevent wide swings in blood flow whenever MAP changes.
    - Example: When MAP increases, vasoconstriction occurs limiting hyper-perfusion of capillaries. When MAP decreases, vasodilatation allows for increased blood flow to organs. However, organs have their limits and autoregulation can fail during hypertensive emergencies.

## Slide 5

- Pathophysiology in Hypertensive Crisis
- Abrupt rise in systemic vascular resistance (SVR) possibly mediated by humoral vasoconstrictors from the wall of a stressed blood vessel
- The endothelium adapts to high blood pressure by smooth muscle hypertrophy and vasoconstriction (essentially protecting the walls of the vessel from the excessive force of blood)
- Prolonged smooth muscle contraction leads to endothelial dysfunction, diminished nitric oxide production and failure of autoregulation
- There will be focal vasodilatation and endothelial damage leading to vascular permeability
- Fibrinoid necrosis
- Activation of the clotting cascade
- Inflammatory factors
- Increasing release of vasoconstrictors (angiotensin II, norepinephrine, vasopressin)

## Slide 6

- HTN Emergency
- End organ damage during hypertensive emergencies falls under three broad categories which are commonly affected:
    - Central nervous system
    - Cardiopulmonary manifestations
    - Renal failure
- Other hypertensive end organ damage from emergencies can affect the eyes, the vasculature, or in the cases of pregnancy, the mom and baby.

## Slide 7

- Neuro Damage in Hypertensive Emergency
- Acute end-organ damage in the setting of HTN emergency may include neurologic damage.
- Patients may develop hypertensive encephalopathy which is characterized by altered mentation/ confusion due to generalized edema of the brain from high hydrostatic pressures and cerebral congestion.

## Slide 8

- Neuro Damage in Hypertensive Emergency
- A second neurologic syndrome caused by hypertensive emergency is posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (PRES).
- PRES symptoms include vision changes, seizures, severe HA and edema of the brain.  AMS may occur.
- This edema most commonly affects the B/L occipital and parietal lobes and is seen best on MRI.

## Slide 15

- CV Damage in Hypertensive Emergency
- Aortic dissection
  - High pressures in the aorta can lead to tears in the intima that widen rapidly.
- [picture]
  - As the blood is forced into the space between the intima and the media, the dissection worsens quickly and can rupture.

## Slide 16

- CV Damage in Hypertensive Emergency
- Myocardial ischemia/infarction (STEMI, NSTEMI) or unstable angina (UA)
  - The heart muscle is perfused by the coronary arteries when it relaxes during diastole.
  - The pressure in the aorta from increased vascular resistance, severely elevated afterload, and hypertrophy of the heart means that the coronary arteries are less likely to perfused.

## Slide 17

- CV Damage in Hypertensive Emergency
- If the heart muscle is too tensely contracted and overly stiff from hypertrophy, then the myocardium both
  - Needs more oxygen for pumping demand and
  - May not relax enough for diastolic filling
- Patients will have troponin elevation and may develop EKG changes.

## Slide 18

- CardioPulm Damage in Hypertensive Emergency
- Acute left ventricular dysfunction causing acute pulmonary edema
  - In hypertensive crisis, the afterload (aortic pressure) becomes so high that forward movement of the blood out of the left ventricle is compromised.
  - Blood backs up into the lungs causing pulmonary vascular congestion that progressively worsens.

## Slide 19

- Renal Damage in Hypertensive Emergency
- Acute kidney injury or proteinuria
  - As the cardiac output falls in the face of severely increased afterload, the kidney will be hypo-perfused and unable to maintain proper filtration to recondition the blood composition.

## Slide 20

- End-Organ Damage in Hypertensive Emergency
- Acute end-organ damage in the setting of a hypertensive emergency may also include:
  - Hypertensive retinopathy: vascular changes within the retina causing exudates and microinfarctions.
  - Pre-eclampsia/ eclampsia: a pregnancy related hypertensive crisis marked by placental insufficiency, proteinuria and potential seizures.
  - Microangiopathic hemolytic anemia: hemolysis as a result of high vascular pressures.

## Slide 24

- Hypertensive Crisis Exam
- Physical exam should assess for end-organ dysfxn
- BP should be measured in supine position and the standing position to assess volume depletion and in both arms as a difference suggests aortic dissection.
  - Pulses: Check for weak or absent carotid, brachial, or femoral pulse as this could suggest aortic dissection as well.

## Slide 27

- Hypertensive Crisis Work-up
- Serum chemistry panel to evaluate for a new AKI by looking at the BUN and Cr, as well as the calculated eGFR.
- Urinalysis (U/A) to detect hematuria, proteinuria, red blood cells (RBCs) or RBC casts.
- CBC, if a new anemia, consider a set of LFTs and a haptoglobin as well as a peripheral blood smear to exclude microangiopathic anemia.

## Slide 28

- Hypertensive Crisis Work-up
- HS troponin now and in 60-90 minutes, if elevated repeat every 6-8 hours x 2.
- Obtain a urine toxicology screen, pregnancy test, and consider endocrine testing if suspicious.
- Imaging should be directed by the patient’s clinical presentation.  Consider a CXR, if dyspnea or chest pain.  Perform a non-contrast CT head for AMS or neurologic changes.

## Slide 29

- Hypertensive Management
- Our concern stems from two competing concerns:
- The potential risk of imminent CV events (aortic dissection, stroke, MI, AKI) that may result from severe hypertension if the blood pressure is not quickly and sufficiently reduced.
- vs
- The risk of adverse events (stroke, MI, AKI) that may occur if the blood pressure is lowered too rapidly or to a level below the ability for autoregulation to maintain adequate tissue perfusion.

## Slide 30

- Hypertensive Urgency Management
- Studies have revealed, that hypertensive urgency patients who present to an ambulatory setting (urgent care, office) do NOT need to be sent to the ER for evaluation and management and should instead be lowered in the outpatient setting.
- Elderly patients are at particular risk of complications if the BP is lowered precipitously.

## Slide 31

- Hypertensive Urgency Treatment
- Just placing the patient in a quiet room to rest for 30min can cause BP to fall ≥ 20/10mmHg in 1/3rd of patients.
- Patients with hypertensive urgency, judged to be at imminent risk of a CV event (known cerebral or aortic aneurysms) should be sent to the ER and can have their BP lowered over a period of 2-4 hours.
    - These patients can be treated as if they have hypertensive emergency, and the same medications used.
- Those with h/o DM, CAD or prior CVA should be considered for admission as well.

## Slide 32

- Hypertensive Urgency Treatment
- If a patient has been non-compliant with previously prescribed and well tolerated treatment, this is restarted- provided that no contraindications exist.
- Salt restriction may also be very helpful.
- Patients with new onset HTN presenting with urgency can be started on appropriate guideline-based therapy-  initiating 2 drugs at modest dosages (ACE or ARB, CCB, thiazide).
- Prompt out-pt follow-up is needed to continue to reduce BP to goal over days to weeks.

## Slide 33

- Specific Strategies for HTN Urgency
- Within 2-4 hours, the BP should be reduced to less than 160/100, or the MAP reduced by no more than 25-30% over 2-4 hours (whichever is greater).  Then over 1-2 weeks the BP is gradually reduced to goal.
- The patient should be monitored for 1-2 hours to make sure that they don’t develop signs of end organ damage.  Patients can be released as BP comes down but must have close outpatient follow-up established.

## Slide 34

- Hypertensive Emergency Treatment
- In hypertensive emergency, the treatment guidelines are to reduce the MAP no more than 10-20% over minutes to 1 hour, using IV medications.
- Then reduce the MAP another 5-15% (from current BP value) over the next 23 hours in the ICU or stepdown unit under close monitoring with IV meds.
- Over the next days to weeks the BP lowering is continued with oral meds.
- There are a few exceptions to this rule in which certain processes require a faster reduction or no reduction in BP at all in the first day. (Slide 47-49)

## Slide 37

- Sodium Nitroprusside (Nipride) IV
- Dilated in 5% Dextrose
- Nitrates Vasodilate
- Nitroprusside will cause a precipitous drop in BP within 2 minutes by causing direct venous and arteriolar vasodilation which equates to decreased PVR and venous return, lowering BP. Nitroprusside raises intracranial pressure (ICP).
- The medication effect lasts up to 10 minutes

## Slide 38

- Sodium Nitroprusside (Nipride) IV
- Sodium nitroprusside has two major adverse rxns:
- Cyanide toxicity: nitroprusside administration causes large quantities of cyanide to be produced.  Humans have limited ability to clear cyanide, especially if they have renal/liver dz. Treatment sodium thiosulfate
- Methemoglobinemia: Hgb is oxidized from the ferrous (Fe2+) to ferric (Fe3+) state and no longer allows binding of oxygen.  Patients develop cyanosis and can die of hypoxia despite high flow oxygen.  Treatment is methylene blue.

## Slide 39

- Nitroglycerin
- The nitroglycerin IV drip is titratable and works immediately to produce a vasodilatory effect on the peripheral veins and a lesser effect on the peripheral arteries.
- Reduces preload, with less reduction of afterload, dilates the coronary arteries and improves collateral flow to areas of ischemic myocardium.
- Onset of action 2-5 min
- Antihypertensive effect disappears in 5-10 minutes

## Slide 40

- Nitroglycerin
- Nitroglycerin causes headache, flushing and hypotension
- Tachyphylaxis develops within 24-48 hours- must have a nitrate free interval
- Concurrent use with PDE-5 inhibitors for erectile dysfunction or pulmonary HTN (sildenafil, tadalafil) may cause very severe hypotension.
- May also cause methemoglobinemia.

## Slide 41

- Nicardipine (Cardene)
- A dihydropyridine CCB in an IV drip, administered through a large peripheral vein or central line, titrated to a goal BP.
- Lowers BP by inhibiting peripheral cardiac calcium channels causing vasodilation.
    - It can affect cardiac calcium channels causing decreased strength of myocardial contraction.
- Cardene may cause irritation of the vein, so site should be rotated every 12 hours.

## Slide 42

- Nicardipine (Cardene)
- Peak BP lowering effect occurs in 1-2 hours and decreases sharply over 30 minutes after infusion stops but it takes about 50 hours for all the anti-hypertensive effect to resolve.
- Effect is prolonged in kidney disease.
- Contraindicated in persons with aortic stenosis, severe ventricular dysfunction (HFrEF) or hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.

## Slide 43

- Hypertensive Emergency: Treating Cardiac Complications
- Acute coronary syndromes, including acute STEMI, NSTEMI and unstable angina.
- Treat if the SBP is >160 mm Hg and/or the DBP is >100 mm Hg.  Nitroglycerin gtt or IV beta blockers (metoprolol, esmolol) are the preferred drugs.
- Get cardiology involved early and trend troponin, obtain an echocardiogram if not going to the cardiac catheterization lab.

## Slide 44

- Hypertensive Emergency: Treating Cardiac Complications
- Acute heart failure, goal is a SBP < 140mmHg
- A loop diuretic IV is often administered first if the patient is fluid overloaded to dry the lungs
- A venous vasodilator- nitroglycerin is added next, which dilates the coronary arteries as well.
- Drugs to avoid in acute decompensated HFrEF are:
  - BBs and CCBs: depress cardiac contractility
  - Hydralazine: increases cardiac work by arterial vasodilation

## Slide 45

- Hypertensive Emergency:Treating Sympathetic Over Activity
- Occurs with the following:
  - Cocaine toxicity
  - Methamphetamines/ Amphetamines (Ritalin, Adderall)
  - Pheochromocytoma
  - Severe autonomic dysfunction: cord injury, GBS
- In these disease states, the sympathetic nervous system is releasing large amounts of catecholamines causing sudden severe HTN

## Slide 46

- Hypertensive Emergency: Treating Sympathetic Over Activity
- In these cases, the patient is quite tachycardic and hypertensive, perhaps with coronary ischemia.
- However, administration of a beta blocker alone is contraindicated, since blocking of the beta receptors will result in all the catecholamine binding to the alpha receptors causing unopposed alpha-adrenergic vasoconstriction and a further rise in blood pressure.
- Block both together with labetalol IV

## Slide 47

- Hypertensive Crisis Treatment Exceptions
- A few exceptions exist for treatment of hypertensive emergency.
- For acute ischemic stroke, treat only if the SBP is ≥ 220 mm Hg and the DBP is ≥ 120 mm Hg: preferred meds are labetalol and nicardipine.
  - All others resume home antihypertensive regimen, pending no contraindications, 24-48hr after the stroke
- For an ischemic stroke patient receiving IV or intra-arterial (IA) fibrinolysis with tPA, goal is <185 and <110 mmHg prior to reperfusion tx.

## Slide 48

- Hypertensive Crisis Treatment Exceptions
- For acute intracerebral hemorrhage or subarachnoid hemorrhage, determine if increased ICP is present.
- Goal BP is a difficult decision to make, with competing interests- why?
- Preferred meds are nicardipine (DOC), could also use labetalol or esmolol; avoid nitroprusside and hydralazine, usually titrated to a systolic of 160.

## Slide 49

- Hypertensive Crisis Treatment Exceptions
- For acute aortic dissection, the goal is to rapidly lower the SBP to 100-120 mm Hg within 20 min, unless signs of end-organ hypoperfusion occur.
- Preferred treatment is a combination of:
  - narcotic analgesics (morphine sulfate)
  - beta blockers (esmolol, labetalol)
  - vasodilators (nitroprusside)
- Then the patient goes to the OR.

## Slide 50

- Hypertension in Pregnancy
- Most women who become pregnant do not have issues with BP, however, the plasma volume increases to 150% of normal during pregnancy, the RBC volume increases by 30% and CV system is under increased stress.  So HTN can occur in pregnancy.
- It is important to differentiate preeclampsia from chronic, transient, & gestational hypertension during pregnancy.

## Slide 51

- Hypertension in Pregnancy
- Chronic hypertension:
  - Pre-pregnancy hypertension (prior diagnosis)
  - > 140/90 mmHg before 20 weeks’ gestation
  - HTN which persists > 12 weeks postpartum
- Pregnancy induced hypertension/ gestational hypertension:
  - > 140/90 after 20 weeks gestation without proteinuria, which resolves by 12 weeks post-partum

## Slide 52

- Hypertension in Pregnancy
- Preeclampsia: hypertension that is either chronic or gestational with placental insufficiency and proteinuria- end organ kidney damage
  - More common in nulliparous women, multiple gestation, in a woman with a h/o HTN or renal disease, family hx
  - Restricted activity and possibly bed rest for mom, very close monitoring of the fetus
  - Definitive treatment of preeclampsia is delivery
- Eclampsia: HTN, proteinuria and seizure

## Slide 53

- Hypertension in Pregnancy
- Methyldopa, labetalol and diltiazem are the drugs used most commonly during pregnancy for hypertensive disorders.
- Labetalol: twice daily, well tolerated, good safety data
- Methyldopa: 2-4 times daily, good safety data, some anti-cholinergic side effects
- Nifedipine or Diltiazem: once daily, less safety data

### Slide 21 (flowchart: McPhee, Pathophysiology of Disease)

- Hypertension → ↑ Afterload → systolic dysfunction; LVH → diastolic dysfunction → heart failure; ↑ myocardial oxygen demand → myocardial ischemia and infarction
- Hypertension → Arterial damage → accelerated atherosclerosis (coronary vessels → ↓ myocardial oxygen supply → ischemia/infarction; cerebral vessels → ischemic stroke) and weakened vessel walls (aorta → aneurysms and dissection; cerebral vessels → hemorrhagic stroke; renal vessels → nephrosclerosis and renal failure; ophthalmic vessels → retinopathy)

### Slide 55 (hypertensive emergency algorithm)

- HTN → End-organ dysfunction? No → Discharge: initiate or intensify antihypertensive regimen
- CNS (AMS, focal neuro deficit, intractable vomiting) → work-up CT head, CTA brain/neck, MRI brain
  - Ischemic stroke → labetalol, nicardipine → thrombolysis candidate BP < 185/110; non-candidate BP < 220/120
  - Intracranial hemorrhage → nicardipine → hemorrhagic stroke SBP < 140; subarachnoid hemorrhage SBP < 160
  - Hypertensive encephalopathy → nicardipine
- Renal (decreased urine output) → BMP, UA → acute hypertensive nephrosclerosis → fenoldopam
- Cardiopulmonary (chest pain, dyspnea, back pain) → CXR, ECG, troponin, CTA chest
  - Acute coronary syndrome → nitroglycerin; acute heart failure → nitroglycerin
  - Aortic dissection → esmolol, nitroprusside → immediately HR < 60; once target HR achieved SBP 100–120
- Encephalopathy / nephrosclerosis / ACS / HF goal: first hour reduce BP 10–20%; first 24 hours reduce an additional 5–15%, or to < 160/110
- Obstetric (seizure, headache, visual disturbances; typically > 20 weeks gestation and < 6 weeks postpartum) → CBC, BMP, LFT, UA, protein:creatinine → preeclampsia or eclampsia → labetalol, hydralazine → immediately SBP < 150, DBP 80–100
